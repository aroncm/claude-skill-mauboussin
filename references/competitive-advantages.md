# Competitive Advantages Framework

## The Five Sources of Competitive Advantage

Based on Mauboussin's "Measuring the Moat" and related works.

### 1. Supply-Side Economies of Scale

**Definition**: Companies that achieve lower unit costs as production volume increases.

**Key Characteristics**:
- High fixed costs, low variable costs
- Minimum efficient scale creates barrier to entry
- Cost advantages are sustainable and difficult to replicate
- Market share matters significantly

**Metrics to Evaluate**:
- Unit cost curve vs. volume
- Fixed cost as % of total costs
- Market share vs. nearest competitors
- Gross margin trends over time
- Operating leverage

**Examples**:
- Manufacturing with expensive equipment
- Distribution networks (warehouses, routes)
- Some technology infrastructure

**Pitfalls**:
- Technology changes can reset scale economics
- Smaller competitors can thrive in niches
- Scale ≠ moat if industry has low fixed costs

### 2. Network Effects (Demand-Side Economies of Scale)

**Definition**: Products or services that become more valuable as more people use them.

**Types**:

**Direct Network Effects**:
- Each additional user directly increases value for all users
- Examples: telephone networks, messaging apps, social media
- Strength: Very powerful, winner-take-most dynamics

**Indirect Network Effects (Two-Sided Networks)**:
- Platforms connecting two or more user groups
- Examples: marketplaces, payment networks, operating systems
- Value increases with both sides of the market

**Data Network Effects**:
- More usage generates more data, improving the product
- Creates virtuous cycle of improvement
- Example: search engines, recommendation systems

**Metrics to Evaluate**:
- User growth rate and trajectory
- Engagement metrics (DAU/MAU ratio)
- Multi-tenanting behavior (do users use competitors too?)
- Cross-side network effects strength
- Retention and churn rates

**Critical Mass Questions**:
- What density of users is needed for value?
- Is the network effect local or global?
- Can network be fragmented?
- What prevents multi-homing?

**Sustainability Assessment**:
- Network effects can weaken over time
- Watch for declining engagement despite user growth
- New platforms can attract users with different value prop
- Regulatory risks to platform business models

### 3. Switching Costs

**Definition**: The pain (financial or otherwise) a customer experiences when changing providers.

**Types of Switching Costs**:

**Financial Switching Costs**:
- Direct costs of switching
- Loss of discounts or loyalty benefits
- New setup or training costs

**Procedural Switching Costs**:
- Time and effort to change
- Learning curve for new system
- Integration with existing processes

**Relational Switching Costs**:
- Relationship with service provider
- Accumulated history and customization
- Trust and reliability

**Data/Integration Switching Costs**:
- Embedded in workflows
- Data migration challenges
- API integrations

**Metrics to Evaluate**:
- Customer retention rate
- Churn rate by cohort
- Net revenue retention (NRR)
- Customer lifetime value
- Gross retention vs. net retention
- Win-back rate of churned customers

**Strength Indicators**:
- Long contract lengths
- High renewal rates at price increases
- Low churn even with competitive pressure
- Increasing share of wallet over time

**Warning Signs**:
- Easy export of data
- Low integration depth
- Commoditized offering
- Price-based renewals only

### 4. Intangible Assets

**Definition**: Non-physical assets that create competitive advantages.

**Categories**:

**Brands**:
- Creates pricing power
- Reduces customer acquisition costs
- Builds trust and reduces perceived risk
- Evaluation: Price premium vs. generics, unprompted awareness, Net Promoter Score

**Patents and Intellectual Property**:
- Legal protection for innovations
- Time-limited but renewable through innovation
- Evaluation: Patent portfolio quality, remaining life, freedom to operate

**Regulatory Licenses**:
- Government-granted barriers to entry
- Stable but subject to policy changes
- Examples: banking licenses, spectrum rights, utilities
- Evaluation: Exclusivity period, regulatory trends, political risk

**Proprietary Data**:
- Unique datasets that are difficult to replicate
- Compound value as data accumulates
- Evaluation: Data uniqueness, accumulation rate, barriers to replication

**Corporate Culture and Processes**:
- Difficult to observe and replicate
- Can enable sustained innovation
- Examples: innovation culture, operational excellence
- Evaluation: Employee retention, innovation output, execution consistency

**Metrics and Evaluation**:
- Brand: Price premium, marketing ROI, brand value
- Patents: R&D efficiency, patent citations, litigation success
- Regulatory: Stability of regulatory framework, renewal likelihood
- Data: Dataset uniqueness, refresh rate, competitive value

### 5. Cost Advantages (Independent of Scale)

**Definition**: Structural cost advantages not related to company size.

**Sources**:
- Proprietary technology
- Preferential access to inputs
- Geographic advantages
- Process innovations
- Learning curve effects

**Evaluation**:
- Gross margin vs competitors
- Cost structure analysis
- Sustainability of advantage
- Threat of disruption

## Measuring Moat Width and Sustainability

### Moat Width

**Wide Moat Indicators**:
- Multiple sources of competitive advantage
- Dominant market share (>50%) in core markets
- Consistent high returns on capital (ROIC > 15%)
- Pricing power demonstrated through price increases
- Long customer relationships (>5 years average)

**Narrow Moat Indicators**:
- One primary source of advantage
- Solid market position but not dominant (20-40%)
- Returns above cost of capital but moderate
- Some pricing power in segments
- Decent customer retention

**No Moat Indicators**:
- No durable competitive advantage
- Commodity-like competition
- Returns on capital at or below cost of capital
- Price taker in the market
- High customer churn

### Moat Trajectory

**Strengthening**:
- Network effects accelerating
- Switching costs increasing
- Market share gains
- Improving unit economics
- Innovation extending advantages

**Stable**:
- Competitive position maintaining
- Returns on capital steady
- Market share stable
- Reinvestment maintaining position

**Weakening**:
- New competitors gaining share
- Pricing pressure increasing
- Technology disruption threats
- Customer concentration decreasing
- Returns on capital declining

## Quantitative Moat Indicators

1. **Return on Invested Capital (ROIC)**: Consistently above WACC
2. **Economic Profit**: Positive and growing economic value added
3. **Gross Margin Stability**: Stable or expanding margins
4. **Market Share Trends**: Gaining or defending share
5. **Customer Metrics**: Low churn, high lifetime value
6. **Pricing Power**: Ability to raise prices above inflation

## Common Mistakes in Moat Analysis

1. **Confusing Size with Moat**: Large companies can have no moat
2. **Assuming Past = Future**: Historical moats can erode
3. **Ignoring Industry Structure**: Some industries structurally can't have moats
4. **Technology Hype**: Claimed "network effects" that don't exist
5. **Ignoring Substitutes**: Moat against current competitors but vulnerable to new categories
6. **Short-term Focus**: Moats matter over 5-10+ year horizons

## Integration with Valuation

A moat's value depends on:
1. **Return on Capital**: Higher returns create more value
2. **Durability**: Longer competitive advantage period
3. **Growth Runway**: Size of addressable market
4. **Reinvestment Opportunities**: Can excess returns be scaled?

The present value of competitive advantage =
(ROIC - WACC) × Invested Capital × Duration × Growth Rate

## Key Questions for Any Moat Analysis

1. What specifically prevents competitors from replicating this business?
2. How long has this advantage existed and how has it changed?
3. What evidence exists of economic returns above cost of capital?
4. Is the advantage getting stronger or weaker?
5. What could eliminate this competitive advantage?
6. How much of current valuation depends on this moat continuing?
7. What are the leading indicators of moat expansion or erosion?
