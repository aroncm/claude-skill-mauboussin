# Expectations Investing Framework

## Core Concept

**Expectations Investing** is a framework developed by Michael Mauboussin and Alfred Rappaport that reverses traditional investment analysis. Instead of estimating value and comparing to price, you derive what expectations are embedded in the current stock price, then assess the likelihood those expectations will be met or revised.

## The Key Insight

> "Stock prices reflect expectations. The goal is to identify where expectations are too high or too low and position accordingly."

Market prices incorporate collective expectations about:
- Future growth rates
- Profit margins
- Returns on capital
- Competitive position
- Market share
- Duration of competitive advantage

## The Three-Step Process

### Step 1: Estimate Current Expectations

**Question**: What future performance is implied by today's stock price?

**Approach**:
1. Take current stock price as given
2. Work backwards to determine implied future performance
3. Use DCF model to reverse-engineer expectations
4. Identify key value drivers and their implied levels

**Key Drivers to Extract**:
- Revenue growth rate (short-term and long-term)
- Operating margin trajectory
- Return on incremental capital
- Investment rate (% of sales)
- Competitive advantage period
- Terminal value assumptions

**Tools**:
- Reverse DCF analysis
- Scenario analysis
- Comparable company analysis
- Historical performance benchmarks

### Step 2: Identify Potential Expectation Changes

**Question**: What could cause expectations to be revised upward or downward?

**Revision Triggers (Positive)**:
- Faster than expected growth in TAM
- Market share gains vs. expectations
- Margin expansion from operating leverage or pricing power
- Successful new product launches
- Competitive dynamics improving
- Capital efficiency improvements
- Multiple expansion catalysts

**Revision Triggers (Negative)**:
- Growth deceleration
- Market share losses or competitive pressure
- Margin compression
- Failed product launches or R&D setbacks
- Deteriorating competitive position
- Capital allocation mistakes
- Multiple contraction risks

### Step 3: Assess Probability of Revision

**Question**: How likely are expectations to change, and in which direction?

**Analytical Framework**:
- Compare implied expectations to base rates
- Evaluate company-specific factors
- Assess industry structure and trends
- Consider management track record
- Analyze competitive dynamics
- Review leading indicators

## Price vs. Value vs. Expectations

**Traditional Framework**:
- Calculate intrinsic value
- Compare to price
- Buy if price < value

**Expectations Framework**:
- Price reflects current expectations
- Value depends on future performance
- Opportunity exists when expectations will likely change

**Key Difference**:
The expectations approach focuses on the *change* in expectations rather than absolute value. This matters because:
1. Markets are forward-looking
2. Returns come from expectation revisions
3. Consensus expectations are often visible
4. Changes are easier to identify than absolute values

## Reading Market Expectations

### From Valuation Multiples

**P/E Ratio**:
- High P/E = high growth or high quality expectations
- Low P/E = low growth or high risk expectations
- Compare to historical range and peers

**EV/Sales or P/S**:
- Reflects expectations for margins and capital efficiency
- Useful for growth companies or turnarounds
- Watch for margin assumption reasonableness

**Price/Book or EV/Invested Capital**:
- Reflects expectations for returns on capital
- High ratios = high ROIC expectations
- Decompose into ROIC and growth expectations

### From Analyst Models

**Sell-side Consensus**:
- Revenue growth by year
- Margin trajectory
- EPS estimates
- Price targets embed expectations

**Variation in Estimates**:
- Wide dispersion = high uncertainty
- Narrow dispersion = strong consensus
- Both create different opportunity types

### From Market Prices

**Implied Volatility**:
- Options prices show uncertainty expectations
- High IV = high expected variance in outcomes

**Credit Spreads**:
- Bond prices reflect default expectations
- Equity may not fully reflect credit market info

## Applying Expectations Analysis

### Growth Companies

**Typical Embedded Expectations**:
- High revenue growth (30%+ for several years)
- Margin expansion as scale achieved
- Large TAM penetration
- Competitive position maintenance

**Common Mistakes**:
- Extrapolating recent growth too far
- Ignoring competition and market saturation
- Underestimating execution risk
- Overestimating margin potential

**What to Analyze**:
- Is the TAM assumption realistic?
- What market share is implied?
- How sustainable is growth at this rate?
- What would cause growth to decelerate?
- Are margins consistent with competitive structure?

### Value/Cyclical Companies

**Typical Embedded Expectations**:
- Low or no growth
- Current cycle conditions persist or worsen
- No multiple expansion
- Capital allocation maintains current patterns

**Common Mistakes**:
- Overly pessimistic on normalized earnings
- Ignoring cycle positioning
- Missing restructuring potential
- Underestimating optionality

**What to Analyze**:
- Where are we in the cycle?
- What are normalized earnings?
- Is market too pessimistic on fundamentals?
- What could drive re-rating?

### Turnaround Situations

**Typical Embedded Expectations**:
- Continued poor performance
- No operational improvement
- Management maintains course
- Market share erosion continues

**What Could Revise Expectations**:
- New management team
- Portfolio rationalization
- Cost restructuring
- Strategic pivot
- Competitive dynamics shift

### Wide-Moat Quality Companies

**Typical Embedded Expectations**:
- Steady growth (GDP+ to low double digits)
- Stable margins
- Consistent returns on capital
- Moat maintenance or gradual expansion

**What Could Revise Expectations**:
- Faster growth from new markets/products
- Margin expansion from scale or mix
- Capital deployment acceleration
- Moat expansion visible
- Conversely: competitive threats, disruption, saturation

## Probabilistic Thinking in Expectations

### Scenario Analysis

Don't think single-point estimate. Think probability distribution.

**Build Scenarios**:
1. **Bull Case** (20% probability): What if things go really well?
2. **Base Case** (50% probability): Most likely outcome
3. **Bear Case** (30% probability): What if disappointed?

**For Each Scenario**:
- Revenue growth trajectory
- Margin path
- Returns on capital
- Resulting stock price range

**Expected Value**:
Weight scenarios by probability to get expected return.

### Sensitivity Analysis

**Key Questions**:
- Which variables matter most?
- How sensitive is value to each driver?
- Where is uncertainty highest?
- Which assumptions are most aggressive?

**Focus Analysis** on highest impact, highest uncertainty variables.

## Expectations and Market Efficiency

### When Expectations Are Likely Mispriced

1. **Structural Complexity**: Hard to analyze businesses
2. **Behavioral Biases**: Recency bias, extrapolation, etc.
3. **Limited Coverage**: Smaller companies, foreign markets
4. **Narrative Dominance**: Story overrides fundamentals
5. **Time Horizon Mismatch**: Long-term value vs short-term trading
6. **Liquidity Constraints**: Forced selling/buying

### When Expectations Are Likely Accurate

1. **High Visibility**: Simple business, extensive coverage
2. **Efficient Markets**: Large cap, liquid, well-followed
3. **Stable Business**: Predictable cash flows, low uncertainty
4. **Recent Information**: Just reported, guidance provided

## Integration with Other Frameworks

### Competitive Advantage Analysis

Expectations often misjudge:
- Moat width (stronger or weaker than perceived)
- Moat trajectory (strengthening/weakening)
- Duration of competitive advantage

### Capital Allocation

Expectations may not fully incorporate:
- Management's capital allocation skill
- Buyback effectiveness at creating value
- M&A track record and discipline
- Reinvestment opportunity set

### Industry Structure

Markets can be slow to recognize:
- Industry structure improvements
- Competitive dynamics shifts
- Regulatory changes impact
- Technology disruption

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Confirmatory Bias**: Seeking only evidence that supports your view
2. **Precision Illusion**: False confidence in exact DCF outputs
3. **Ignoring Base Rates**: Your specific case vs. historical patterns
4. **Overconfidence**: Certainty that you're right and market wrong
5. **Anchoring**: Getting stuck on past price levels
6. **Narrative Seduction**: Compelling story overrides analysis

## Practical Application

### Before Making Investment

1. What growth, margins, and returns are in the price?
2. How do these compare to history and peers?
3. What has to go right for this to work?
4. What would cause expectations to be revised?
5. How probable is revision vs. current expectations?
6. What is the expected value of various scenarios?
7. What is my edge in seeing this differently?

### Monitoring Positions

1. Are results tracking expectations?
2. Have expectations in the price changed?
3. Are my original assumptions still valid?
4. What new information matters?
5. Do I still have an edge?

### Selling Discipline

Sell when:
1. Original expectations thesis plays out (price now reflects it)
2. Expectations were wrong (new information changes view)
3. Better opportunities exist elsewhere
4. Risk/reward no longer attractive

## Key Takeaways

1. **Focus on expectations, not just value**: Returns come from expectation revisions
2. **Reverse engineer the price**: Understand what's priced in before forming a view
3. **Think probabilistically**: Range of outcomes, not point estimates
4. **Identify catalysts**: What would cause expectations to change?
5. **Be humble**: Market has information you might not
6. **Compare to base rates**: Is this situation likely different from historical patterns?
7. **Monitor and update**: Expectations and fundamentals evolve

Remember: **The question is not "What is this company worth?" but rather "What does the market expect and how likely are those expectations to change?"**
